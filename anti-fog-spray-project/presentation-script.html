"Good morning respected teachers and evaluators. My name is Yadnesh Sadavarte (Roll No. 01), and together with my team members Harshal and Aditya, we have developed FogZero—an anti-fog spray formulation.

The Problem: We frequently experience fogging on eyeglasses, vehicle mirrors, and bathroom glass. When warm water vapor or human breath touches cold glass, natural surface tension causes water molecules to pull together into millions of microscopic, round droplets. Each tiny droplet acts like a tiny convex lens, scattering light in all directions, which results in cloudy, blinded vision.

The Chemical Solution: To solve this, we used Polysorbate 80 (also known as Tween 80). It is a non-ionic, water-soluble surfactant. Polysorbate 80 drastically lowers the surface tension of water. Instead of beading up into scattering droplets, condensing steam immediately flattens out into an ultra-thin, continuous, invisible sheet of water. Because it forms a flat sheet, light passes straight through without scattering, keeping the glass 100% transparent.

Now, I hand over to Harshal to explain our chemical recipe and preparation steps."

"Thank you, Yadnesh. My name is Harshal Bhange (Roll No. 02). I will walk you through our laboratory recipe and synthesis procedure.

Our 100 mL Prototype Recipe: We formulated the solution with three calibrated ingredients:
• Distilled Water (98.5 mL): Serves as the pure carrier base that distributes the surfactant without leaving mineral spots.
• Polysorbate 80 (0.5 mL): The active surfactant that creates the anti-fog water sheet.
• Glycerin (1.0 mL): A humectant that holds moisture so the coating does not dry out too quickly or leave streaks.

As shown on our portal, we built an interactive Batch Scaler that automatically calculates exact ingredient volumes for any bottle size—such as 50 mL travel sprays or 500 mL dispensers—with 100% volumetric balance.

Our 5-Step Lab Procedure: First, we clean a 150 mL Borosil beaker. Second, we add 80 mL distilled water. Third, we slowly measure and add 0.5 mL Polysorbate 80, stirring gently with a glass rod so no foam or air bubbles form. Fourth, we add 1.0 mL glycerin and stir for 2 to 3 minutes until completely mixed. Finally, we top up with water to the 100 mL mark and allow it to rest for 30 minutes to verify zero cloudiness.

Now, Aditya will present our video evidence and steam testing results."

Steam Exposure Testing: To prove that FogZero works, we conducted a dual-slide comparison test over an 85°C boiling steam chamber.
• We left one glass slide untreated as a control.
• We applied a thin coat of FogZero to the second slide.

The Results: The untreated slide fogged up in less than 2 seconds, becoming completely opaque and unusable. In contrast, the FogZero-treated slide stayed 100% crystal-clear. The steam condensed into a flat, transparent film without any fogging, blurring, or distortion. We repeated this test three times under identical conditions with 100% consistent success.

Conclusion: FogZero is a practical, non-toxic, and cost-effective formulation suitable for mirrors, vehicle glass, and lab equipment.

Thank you, teachers and evaluators. We are now happy to answer any questions!"

💡 Quick Reference for Teacher / Evaluator Questions:
  • Q: Why Polysorbate 80 instead of dish soap? A: Dish soap contains ionic surfactants and additives that leave greasy rainbow streaks. Polysorbate 80 is a non-ionic surfactant that dries water-clear and streak-free.
  • Q: Why add Glycerin? A: Glycerin is a humectant. It attracts and retains a tiny moisture buffer, keeping the hydrophilic surfactant layer active for longer.
  • Q: Why did you stir gently? A: Polysorbate 80 is a surfactant, so vigorous shaking creates microscopic foam bubbles that ruin optical clarity and volumetric measurement.
